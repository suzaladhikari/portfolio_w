About

Who I am

I'm an undergraduate researcher at Caldwell University pursuing a B.S. in Computer Science and Mathematics, with concentrations in AI, Machine Learning, and Data Science. My work sits at the intersection of machine learning, mathematical reasoning, and building practical intelligent systems.

My research interests include federated learning, AI security, and privacy-preserving machine learning. At C-STEM Laboratories, I've been benchmarking federated optimization algorithms — FedAvg, FedProx, and FedNova — for network intrusion detection under heterogeneous, non-IID data splits.

I'm also building a deeper understanding of modern deep learning architectures, particularly Transformers and large language models, working through them from the ground up — tokenization, embeddings, self-attention, multi-head attention, and language modeling — while connecting the underlying mathematics to practical implementations.

Beyond research, I enjoy building end-to-end ML systems that move past experimentation into practical deployment. Recent work includes research on privacy-preserving federated learning for network intrusion detection, a decoder-only Transformer built from scratch in PyTorch, and a distributed event-processing pipeline deployed on AWS using FastAPI, SQS, RDS, and ECS Fargate. I focus on building intelligent systems that are reproducible, scalable, and effective under real-world conditions.

I also lead outreach for the Google Developer Groups chapter at Caldwell University, where I help organize AI/ML workshops, hackathons, and technical events for a growing student community. Outside of technical work, I enjoy fitness, reading, exploring new places, and learning through philosophy and religion.
